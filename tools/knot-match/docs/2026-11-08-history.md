# Celtic knotwork border generator: design history and synthesis (July to November 2026)

This is a read-only reconstruction of how the ORK3 knotwork border engine (`scroll-knot.js`) and its matching tool (`tools/knot-match/`) came to be, written to inform a variation phase: tighter or looser weaves, thicker or thinner bands, other patterns, slider configuration, and replicating arbitrary reference art.

**Sources.** The knot memory notes; the July spec and plan; about 130 commits on `scroll-knot.js`, `tools/test-scroll-knot.cjs` and `tools/knot-match`; the prior session's scratch `km/` (ledger, six visual reviews, two outside reviews, perf bench, round dirs); the review artifact's HTML (https://claude.ai/artifact/VRxZ6bWubAez78Wr1aTztf); the owner's messages in transcripts `8e4c23d4…` (Nov 6–8) and `e0418485…`/`1407c198…` (Oct 27); Ash's `knot.svg`; and code checks against `knot-v6-engine` @ 1ef5b0ddd.

**Conventions.** Times are local as committed (EDT July, EST November; transcript UTC converted). **Metric v1** (Nov 7) is IoU plus chamfer with a free colour swap per region. **Metric v2** (from Nov 8 01:00) uses a fixed colour mapping and a weighted confusion score. **Joint page** means one config on one engine over the whole page, the only number that counts as a deliverable.

**Gap.** No July transcripts exist on disk (the oldest is Oct 10), so July owner verdicts come only from commit messages and the memory note, and are paraphrases unless marked as quotes.

## 0. The target: Ash's Jovious knot

`assets/scrolls/common/knot/knot.svg` (`feature/scrollery`) was exported from the Arcanyst Affinity source on 2026-10-27 (master "Overlay (Jovious)", band groups `/3/8` and `/3/9`).

**The file.**
- Two `<symbol>`s on a 2550×3300 page: `Knot-Band-1` (colour 1, blue) and `Knot-Band-2` (colour 2, pink).
- 636 even-odd filled paths: 318 black outline shapes, 158 colour-1 fills and 160 colour-2 fills.
- So the art is about 318 visible fragments between crossings, each drawn as a filled shape with its own ink shape. **It is a filled-shape drawing, not a stroked one.** That is why a constant-width stroke model later capped out near 0.85 (§1.11).

**Structure.**
- **Weave.** A 4-strand plait in 2 colours, two strands per colour, alternating. Read along any strand, the over/under is strictly O-U-O-U.
  - Visually it is edge arcs that hug the band edges, crossed by short straight centre bars.
  - The two diagonal families are not alike: one family is straight bars, and the other bows out to hug the edge (the "Ash bow").
  - Arc tips taper to points at the band edge.
  - Under-strands are cut with a clear gap of ground each side of the over-strand. Their cut faces run parallel to the over-strand's edge, because the under-strand curls in over its last stretch to meet it at 90°.
- **Proportions,** at the tool's half-scale raster (1275×1650):
  - repeat 181 px, about 2.33 band widths;
  - fill about 0.096 band;
  - ink about 0.52× fill per side (Ash measured 29 px fill and 15 px outline at 5100 px);
  - clear gap about 0.53× fill.
- **Layout.** The border is broken at top-centre and bottom-centre, so it is two "[" halves. **The halves are left-right mirror images, not rotations:**
  - Ash against his own L-R mirror scores 0.83–0.89 (v1) and 0.88–0.92 (v2).
  - Ash against his 180° rotation scores 0.33–0.46.
- **Corners (TL).**
  - A pink outer L with a crisp square corner and straight legs along both outer edges.
  - A blue nested L, also square, inside it.
  - Bars crossing the corner diagonal, with long straight diagonals running into the corner's nose.
  - The bottom corners are not rotations of TL in colour: on my reading of the crops, Ash's BL outer L is blue. Visual review 3 recorded it as pink, so check before relying on it.
- **Ends (top-L).**
  - The outer strand (blue) wraps the end as a squared **D**: a flat vertical outer face, a sharp top-outer corner, reaching about 0.4 band further into the break than our first tiles.
  - The inner strand (pink) is a **hairpin**: it turns back on itself, dives under its own incoming leg (a thin slit) and continues back into the weave.
  - There are no free ends anywhere; the knot is contiguous.

## 1. Chronological narrative

### 1.0 Prehistory (April to July 2)

Static "Hibernian Knotwork" SVG assets (Apr 27, 216104cd5) and a Scroll Forge generator (Jul 2, 7e65e2c2e, porting the legacy `celticknot.js` into corner/edge/medallion SVG modules) were excised with the Scroll Forge on Jul 2–3. Their idea survived: a border built from **modules**.

### 1.1 Phase A: spec and module engine (Jul 4, 22:35 → 23:55)

**Hypothesis** (spec 7dd67e43d, "Approach C, procedural tile modules"):
- Pattern modules emit strand polylines per edge segment.
- Corner, terminal and medallion modules join them at shared strand "ports".
- Interlace is a **two-pass painted** effect: all outlines, then all fills, then per-crossing over-arc redraws with a widened dark casing.
- The engine is a live parametric `knot` JSON layer rendered as inline SVG, PDF-safe: no masks, filters or clipPath, so html2canvas can rasterise it.

**Built in about an hour by subagents:** geometry core (667114fe3, 225 lines), renderer, plait/openweave/twist/runningknot patterns, woven/hook corners, diamond medallions, and the designer Border panel with Flame/Dragon/Crown/Smith presets (1c0394531).

**Outcome.** E2E QA found the `knot` config was never saved (0b9296f89). A fix wave (a7b2c125c) cured Smith's sides going solid brown when a terminal U-turn merged two colours, and "bead artifacts" at shallow crossings.

### 1.2 Phase B: spine sweep and true cut gaps (Jul 5)

**Hypothesis:** corner modules joined at ports look assembled. One continuous braid swept around a rounded-rectangle **spine** (corner radius 0.75 band) would flow through corners with no seams.

**Built:**
- **Spine sweep** (986333a47): (u,v) polylines swept along the spine's arc length; one closed loop without breaks, and breaks as arc-length intervals.
- **True cut gaps** (2e928e587): under-strands are geometrically split around each crossing so the page shows through, replacing the painted casing. runningknot removed.
- **Tangent fusion** (052697404): crossings with sin θ < 0.25 merge with no hole.
- **`strands.gap` removed** (74768b134): holes sized to the over-strand footprint, `(wOut/2)/max(0.35, sinA)`.

**Outcome:** a contiguous, PDF-safe interlace. Both the sweep and the true cuts survive today.

### 1.3 Phase C: medallions (Jul 4–5)

**Hypothesis:** diamond lozenges in mid-edge can hold emblems, joined to the braid.

Three connection designs were tried in one day:
1. sweep landings (986333a47);
2. a symmetric vertex funnel (f21b75889, polished in db2ca5d92);
3. a lozenge overlaying the continuous weave (5fab1c71f), then composed inward (85989f9dd).

**Owner verdict:** the medallion concept was removed entirely at 16:37 (09363a3f8). The spec amendment says "three connection designs … all failed the visual bar". Emblems became ordinary slots, with `break_border` for a gap.

### 1.4 Phase D: terminals and corners (Jul 5–6)

**Hypothesis 1:** the reference "hook" corners and rolled terminals are curl motifs.
- Built pointed mitred corners plus rolled hook terminals (eb1dccc51, 17:08), then a settle-then-coil polish (da8482e90).
- **Owner, 18:20 (d1df9ab97):** knots must stay contiguous. The memory note quotes it as "knots should always be contiguous". Open-ended terminals were deleted, and `corners:'hook'` was ruled a misreading and mapped to `'pointed'`.

**Hypothesis 2:** easing strands to a fixed terminal line ("port easing") gives clean ends. This was replaced the same evening by **natural-fold caps** (49b4c2895). Each strand pair is trimmed where the two share a tangent and bridged with a slope-sheared semicircle.

**Hypothesis 3 (Jul 6, from a "4-expert deep-dive"): zero-shear terminals** (4e69fb020).
- Diagnosis: the shear was itself the protruding "hook". The identity is that `s=0` holds exactly when the strand runs parallel to the run at the fold.
- Even N: pair strand `i` with its centreline mirror `i+N/2` (not its nesting partner `N−1−i`), so each fold lands on `v'=0`. The fix had to be made at three pairing sites.
- Odd N: a tangent-matched pointed leaf on the middle strand's symmetry axis.
- `PAIR_STAGGER` went from 0.3 to 0.2 lam, below the natural lam/4 interleave. The owner had caught "red ends early" (an outer pair soloing extra loops).
- The owner chose equal-diameter folds over graduated nesting.
- Permanence invariants pinned it: 24,854 checks.

**Outcome:** a technically sound, contiguous, mathematically clean engine.

**Owner verdict (later, Oct 27 21:39,** while building a border-variants page for the Scrollery): "I don't want the legacy knotwork here just yet (it never looked right.)"

### 1.5 Interlude: the hand-drawn reference arrives (Oct 27)

The owner asked to extract the Jovious border from the Affinity source and recolour it: "the celtic knot border is pretty simple there. Just two bands, unadorned with sidebar elements".

`knot.svg` became the Scrollery's "Custom Border" (4eaa2f91f, 2009f408b). So the product already had a hand-drawn knot rendered as literal art, while the parametric engine sat unmerged on `feature/scroll-generator`.

### 1.6 Phase E: Round 0 and Round 1, the hand-drawn weave (Nov 6, 20:06 → 20:19)

**Request (20:06):** "see what you can do to get the output of the knotwork generator to more closely resemble the hand-drawn artwork."

**Round 0 baseline:** plait-4, thickness 0.55, pointed corners. "Its sinusoidal strands cross at shallow angles, the per-pair over/under breaks the weave, and the thin round-capped outlines run together into black blobs."

**Hypothesis:** three structural differences explain most of the gap: the waveform, the alternation rule and the cut-end shape.

**Built** (63c783aa8):
- **`asin(k·sin θ)` waveform** (`strands.angular`): a rounded triangle wave with straight diagonals and tight turns. Its peaks keep `v'=0`, so zero-shear folds survive.
- **Global alternation:** BFS parity over all crossings along each strand. It replaced per-pair alternation, which had produced a "muddy lattice".
- **Square inked cut ends:** butt caps, fill trimmed by the outline width, and a `GAP_RATIO` clear gap.
- Outline 0.5× fill, where it had been 0.22×.
- The plait repeat scales with N (4-strand ≈ 2.4 band).

**Owner (20:19):** "Hand drawn weave is SIGNIFICANTLY better than previous attempts. As you work on round two, think about alternative weaves, alternative corners, and alternative ends."

### 1.7 Phase F: Round 2 alternatives, then Round 3 designed tiles (Nov 6, 20:19 → 21:54)

**Weaves** (d5f4d6554):
- **Ash bow** (`strands.bow`): one diagonal family stays straight while the other relaxes to a sine, then to an edge-hugging tanh. The peaks hold, so the folds stay zero-shear.
- Also chain, plait-6, and retunes of twist/plait-2 (which had inked into "a black zig-zag") and openweave.
- **Owner (20:39):** "Ash bow looks great, B twist looks good, D plait looks okay if we can make it three colors." Chain ("busy, kinks at corners") and plait-6 ("a mosaic") were proposed for dropping.

**Corners** (f653d9449):
- Fixed the pointed-mitre fragments: inner-offset samples had overshot the miter diagonal and doubled back.
- Added phase-locking so all four corners match.
- Added `'square'` (outer L plus nested L, closest to Ash) and `'round'` (cleanest).
- **Owner (20:53):** "None of these corners are sufficient. If needed, let's figure out how to create corner joins that take two pieces of well constructed braid and using a semi-fixed pattern join them together. Aka make good looking, standalone corners that are not necessarily algorithmically generated." Then (20:54): "I am not opposed to any component parts being fixed and only lightly dynamic (aka allowing for some thickening/gap resizing, but not the whole thing algorithmic). Corners, ends, etc."
- This reversed the July rejection of corner modules.

**Ends** (b14eb865d):
- `ends: fold|square|point|spiral`, plus plait-3 wrap-and-tuck ends.
- A first `'ash'` tile traced from Ash's end, stretched 0.85–1.3× onto a matched weave phase (f856bba1e).
- **Owner (21:20):** "I'm not good with any of those ends. Do intentionally designed tiles for ends instead of algorithmic." The spiral had read "as a scroll flourish rather than Ash's enclosed hook".

**Round 3: designed tiles.**
- **Corner tiles** (e81c00d91): hand-authored centreline data in band units, with ports at a fixed weave phase.
  - Each run is pinned to whole repeats at the tile's port phase, cut at the port lines and G1-snapped into one continuous strand.
  - Tiles are rotated into all four corners. Only weights and gaps stay parametric.
- **End tiles** (28b83b97a): ash, loop and heart, placed undeformed. The run adjusts its repeat by at most 12% (`TILE_LAM_SLACK`) and shifts its phase to land on the tile.
  - "A tile path always takes the colour of the strand coming into it, so colour can only change where a strand passes under another."
  - All algorithmic ends were deleted; `fold` survives only as a silent fallback.
- **The first Ash corner tile was "rough".** Its outer L stopped in an unexplained cut, lead-ins kinked and gaps were uneven.
  - After a cleanup it still "reads sparse and disconnected".
  - Diagnosis: "our braid repeats at 2.17 band widths and Ash's at 2.4, so his corner can't fit our ports."
  - This is the first appearance of the fact that **a tile is only valid at the λ it was drawn for.**

### 1.8 Phase G: the knot-match optimiser, V1–V3 (Nov 7, 19:55 → 23:17; metric v1, rounds 1–10)

**Request (19:55):** "create a script or tool that can visually compare the tiles you have made to the original examples, mathematically analyze the comparison … progressively solve for the closest recreation … until you see no statistically significant improvement in five generations."

**Built in sequence:**

- **Scoring** (52325b344, 20:08).
  - Render through the real engine, rasterise with rsvg at 1275×1650, and classify each pixel as ink, A, B or ground.
  - Score per region after an alignment search: fill, ink and silhouette IoU plus chamfer.
  - Search with a (μ+λ)-ES and a Welch-test stop.
  - Round 1, weave: 0.309 → 0.584, with the **left side at 0.748 and the right at 0.42.**
  - **Owner (20:22):** "This is great. The side bar generation is worlds better."
- **CMA-ES with IPOP restarts** (d04608282). The stop test was moved to pooled 5-generation windows, because single-generation elites lacked power.
- **Mirror symmetry** (1c0df41b2, 20:53).
  - Scoring Ash against his own mirror explained the left/right gap. The engine sweeps clockwise, so its right half is a rotation; Ash's is a mirror.
  - `symmetry:'mirror'` draws the left half and reflects it, cuts and over/under included.
  - With no retuning: sides 0.584 → 0.746, ends 0.513 → 0.628, corner 0.409 → 0.445.
  - The same self-match calibrates the metric's **ceiling**.
- **"Bigger swings"** (owner, 20:54: "take bigger swings when it starts to detect slowing gains, perhaps even … rounds that seed quite differently").
  - Built as kicks plus local, swing and scatter restarts (e5c94233d). The kicks compounded and were capped within 20 minutes (4c14f5bb8).
  - A probe then showed **"the optimum is a ridge, not a basin"** (742f54b2b): σ 0.005–0.015 held the corner at 0.440–0.446, while σ 0.1 dropped it to 0.36–0.42. σ0 was cut to 0.03.
- **One primary region** (owner, 20:59): "ultimately I'm okay if we get one of the corners or ends right and that can be mirrored to all four … Pick one as the primary target."
  - Implemented as `--primary` (TL corner, top-L end).
  - A **four-position gate** checks the other three positions for correctness (kinks, out-of-band, alternation, flecks), not for similarity (c0d43bd4c).
- **Tracing.** `trace-corner.cjs` (5a4cf70de) skeletonises Ash's corner fills and links them across under-gaps.
  - The traced tile scored 0.564 before optimisation, against 0.467 for the best optimised tile. With its own in-port phase it became `ash-traced` (ccc01c641, TL 0.603 → 0.667).
  - **Owner (22:49):** "Your new corner is WAY more improved. Way to go!"
- **Hairpin** (owner, 22:16): "In the endcap, notice how the inner strand turns back around on itself to continue back through the weave."
  - Built as the `ash-hairpin` end tile (a48eb5e02, 72aa1dc95): port → into the C → folds back → under its own incoming leg → next port.
  - Fold region 0.47 → 0.66, gated by `hpX` (exactly one self-crossing) and `hpSlit` (the legs never fuse).
- **Side cut ends** (owner, 22:48, with a screenshot): "notice how Ash sides curl in slightly to make the 'split' run perpendicular to where the cross over/under would be. This is where you are seeing slight mismatches just at the end of your segments."
  - Built as `strands.cutCurl` (f2ac13043): sides 0.7573 → 0.7644 robust, with no refit.
- **Pinned repeat** (`--repeatPx 181`). "Lower but honest": sides scored 0.757, where a ±4% period stretch had given 0.773.

### 1.10 Phase I: the perf workstream (Nov 7 20:27 → Nov 8 02:38)

It ran as `knot-perf{,2,3,4}-*` worktrees, merged when validated. Every change was proven exact (byte-identical SVG over 73–592 renders), or rank-identical for the one opt-in approximation.

- **Engine:**
  - grid-indexed `findCrossings`, 11× (145.8 s → 13.3 s over 73 configs; the quadratic scan had been about 90% of render time, ab57ff038);
  - each polyline measured once per paint, −36%;
  - an all-strands CSR grid, −13 to −16%;
  - crossPairs, −7%;
  - no discarded braid behind end tiles.
- **Tool:**
  - an in-process JS rasteriser reproducing cairo's stroke model (99.84–99.95% pixel agreement with rsvg, cffcf4802);
  - engine globals bound in the vm context, render −64% (347b71c67);
  - bit-mask alignment, −43%;
  - an integer chamfer;
  - opt-in `js:7`, −28%, not exact.
- **Bench, ms per evaluation** (`bench.cjs`):
  - weave 390 (V4, rsvg) → 71 (perf3, exact) → 58 (`js:7`);
  - corner 255 → 57 → 37;
  - end 259 → 45 → 30.
  That is roughly 4.5–6× exact and 7–9× with `js:7`, under varying load (±20%).

### 1.11 Phase J: outside review 1 (Nov 8, ~02:27; after the V8 stall)

The stall rule fired: sides gained less than +0.01 for three cycles. An outside agent measured instead of opining (`km/expt/review.md`).

- **The corner was solver-limited.**
  - The incumbent sat on a narrow ridge. CMA-ES at σ≈0.03–0.04 sampled 0.13–0.18 below the best; corner13 and corner14 together spent about 20k renders for +0.000.
  - An 80-eval (1+1)-ES at σ 0.003 lifted TL from 0.7307 to 0.7501.
- **The sides were representation-limited.**
  - Ash's own centrelines, redrawn as constant-width round-cap strokes, score only about 0.83–0.86.
  - Variable width (Ash's own radius) raises that bound to about 0.87.
- **The boxes are mostly legs.** 60–80% of the TL box is leg braid. Pasting Ash's own 1.5-band corner pixels into our page still scored only 0.74.
  - Fixing the whole top-left segment (corner + top run + end, a 6.7-band piece) scored TL 0.87–0.91 and top-L ≈1.0.
- **About 20% of "pink" pixels in both drawings are antialiased edges.** A coverage-aware classifier adds about 0.025 to everyone, the ceiling included.
- **Stage bests are mutually incompatible.** The corner13 page scores 0.47 on the sides.

**Adopted in V9:**
- the small-step polish;
- painter capability (slanted cut faces, mitre, then filled variable width in V10);
- the joint page as the only deliverable.

**Deferred to the owner:** (a) using Ash's literal art as the TL segment; (b) the coverage classifier.

### 1.12 Phase K: outside review 2 and the per-point fit; goal met (Nov 8 ~05:00 → 05:50)

V12 and V13 both gained less than +0.01 on TL and top-L, so the stall rule fired again (`results/2026-11-08-review-2.md`).

**Winner's curse.** For the V13 incumbent e2, the nominal TL, top-L and left scores were each the **maximum of 16 quarter-pixel phases**, about +0.02 of luck. "The CMA-ES and polish work of V12 and V13 was partly fitting the pixel grid." That explains V13's "noise floor".

**Representation, not engine.** The few smooth modes (6 per path, plus an 18-parameter 2-D warp, plus fold controls) could not express 1–2 px local outline moves.

`fit.cjs` does coordinate descent instead:
- normal offsets at knots every 0.15 band along each tile centreline, blended with raised cosines;
- ports pinned;
- δ = 0.03 → 0.015 → 0.008 band;
- objective = the mean over 4 half-pixel shifts of Ash's raster.

Results:
- **TL 0.843 → 0.867** (2,011 renders, about 5 min).
- **Top-L 0.840 → 0.868** (1,083 renders, about 3 min).
- Joint 0.861.
- The gain held at unseen sub-pixel phases and at 2× resolution.
- It passes every gate, though wrapDent rose 2 → 11, inside the hull-depth tolerance.

Committed as a6a58a5f6 with `results/2026-11-08-joint-best.json`; the loop stopped.

**Owner (08:35):** "This looks much better. You can go ahead and kill all background processes and agents…"

## 2. Catalogue of rejected approaches

**Key to the third column:**
- **F** = fundamental: the idea cannot meet the bar.
- **I** = implementation: the idea might work, built differently.
- **A** = aesthetic veto by the owner.

| Approach | When | Why it failed | F / I / A |
|---|---|---|---|

## 4. The owner's aesthetic principles: acceptance criteria for any variation system

**Stated:**

1. **Contiguous.** Every strand loops back into the band, with no free or open ends. ("knots should always be contiguous", Jul 5.) Ash's apparent spiral is a hairpin, which is consistent with this.
2. **Designed over algorithmic for components.** Corners and ends are "good looking, standalone … not necessarily algorithmically generated". Components may be "fixed and only lightly dynamic (aka allowing for some thickening/gap resizing, but not the whole thing algorithmic)".
3. **Hairpin end.** The inner strand "turns back around on itself to continue back through the weave". Never a free hook.
4. **Cut ends meet the over-strand at 90°.** The under-strand curls in so "the 'split' run[s] perpendicular to where the cross over/under would be".
5. **Colour over black.** "Us putting black somewhere Ash puts color is also a problem." The disagreements that matter are black vs colour, black vs empty and colour vs empty.
6. **Colour fidelity by role.** Colour A always lands where the reference's A is, even if the hues differ. Colour changes only where a strand passes under another (Round 3 rule).
7. **One correct primary, everywhere correct.** One corner and one end must match; the other positions must be *correct* (clean), not necessarily identical.
8. **Fluid and smooth** (Nov 8 synthesis request). This rules out kinks at ports, wobble, bumps and hooks; the zoom-only leftovers are the remaining violations.

**Implied by vetoes and reviews:**

9. **Straight diagonals, tight turns, true alternation (O-U-O).** The sinusoid lattice was "muddy".
10. **Inked outlines with a clear gap** of ground at every cut; no merged "black blobs".
11. **Square corners:** outer L plus nested L with straight legs and a crisp point. Rounded, mitred-busy and floating corners were all vetoed.
12. **Squared-D ends** with a flat outer face and sharp corner. Round "O" rings read wrong at page scale.
13. **Uniform density:** no stretched or unwoven runs anywhere on the page.
14. **Compact, equal-diameter folds;** no outer pair soloing extra loops ("red ends early").
15. **Containment:** nothing protrudes past the band; no flecks or slivers.
16. **Mirror symmetry** where the reference has it (adopted from Ash, not stated as a rule).

**Technical constraints:**

17. PDF-safe SVG: paths, groups and userSpaceOnUse gradients only; no masks, filters or clipPath.
18. Live and parametric: rendered client-side, fast enough for slider input.

## 5. Process lessons

1. **The scoreboard can lie about the rest of the page.** The V9 joint page had bottom runs stretched 2.93× while its scores rose, because only TL and top-L were scored. Visual review 5 caught it. **Every unscored region needs a hard correctness gate** (run-stretch, four-position, endFallback). The owner's "visual review after every best set" paid for itself repeatedly:
   - "more black";
   - the blue wrap;
   - the "O" rings;
   - the bottom stretch;
   - the legs limiting the corner.
2. **Silent fallbacks inflate scores.** The V7 joint's 0.729 drew fold caps because `repeatLam` left the tile's 12% slack. Gate on "the intended component was actually drawn".
3. **Stage pages distort run lengths.** The corner stage's legs ran at 168/188 px against Ash's 179/181, and far runs on corner and end pages were compressed about 11%. Tiles fitted there do not transfer. **Fit on the deliverable page.**
4. **Winner's curse from sub-pixel phase.** Selecting on nominal scores picks phase-lucky designs: e2's nominal was the 16-phase max, worth about +0.02. Search on a jittered mean and report nominal. This explained V12–V13's "noise floor".
5. **Know the landscape before choosing the optimiser.** Tile-shape optima were thin ridges: σ 0.03 lost 0.18, while σ 0.003 gained. Weave optima sat beside discrete cliffs (0.839 → 0.555 at a 0.006 step). Wide CMA and kicks wasted about 20k renders. Topology-level diversity helps; continuous-σ swings hurt.
6. **Representation limits look like solver stalls.**
   - The stroke model capped sides at about 0.85, and the few-mode tile parameterisation capped TL and top-L at about 0.84.
   - Both were found by an *outside* reviewer who measured an upper bound with Ash's own centrelines and a paste oracle, rather than tuning further.
   - Two outside reviews broke the two longest stalls. Each spent its effort measuring, not opining.
7. **Calibrate the metric against the reference itself.** Ash against his own mirror gave a ceiling (0.88–0.92 under v2) and exposed the mirror symmetry in one experiment. Ash against his 180° rotation (0.33–0.46) showed a rotation-swept engine could never fit.
8. **Metrics encode taste.** Metric v1 let each region swap colours, so a pink wrap scored the same as a blue one; the eye objected. Metric v2's fixed mapping and weighted confusion came from the owner.
9. **A JIT can be the bug.** Node 26 / V8 14.6 Maglev miscompiled the hot scoring loops under load: one region came back with another's score, 10–17 times per about 3k jobs (end10's "0.6197" was really 0.5928). The fix was `--no-maglev` plus job-hash echoes and fresh-engine audits (c4352f3f0); the V7 round's audits then found 0 mismatches in 28,056 jobs.
10. **Launch bugs masquerade as stalls.**
    - chain17 and chain18 were zsh array scripts run under bash, so whole rounds ran a bare `--sigma` with no polish.
    - The V7 weave round resumed from the wrong `--cfg`.
    - Carried tile paths grew 8× per pass, and tiles rendered differently (0.445 → 0.383).
11. **Byte-identical opt-in discipline made parallel streams safe.** Every V4–V12 feature defaulted off and was proven byte-identical on a growing set of 73 → 383 configs. The cost was about 30 accumulated opt-in keys (§6).
12. **Parallel dev, perf and rounds** (owner-directed) took V4 to the goal in about 6.5 hours and the whole optimiser in about 10. What worked: ≤2 workers for dev smokes, a merge after each round, a standing bench.
13. **Evaluate sharp geometry exactly.** The asin peaks broke 3-point interpolation at folds and faked a 26° slope, until patterns carried an exact `v(u)` (6de8ab720).

## 6. Open questions and risks for the variation phase

### 6.1 Owner questions still open

- **(a) Ash's literal TL segment as a fixed piece.** The reviewer recommends dropping it, since its seams lower TL to 0.848.
- **(b) A coverage-aware classifier.** About +0.025 for everyone; if adopted, the target must be rebased.
- **Merge path.**
  - Does the fitted engine replace the July engine on `feature/scroll-generator`?
  - Does it replace Ash's literal SVG in the Scrollery Custom Border?
  - Or do both coexist?
- **Default look.** All V4–V12 features are off by default, so a bare config still renders the July look.
- **Cosmetic leftovers** (wrap shoulder bump, nested-L hook). The uncommitted `SMOOTH` regulariser in `fit.cjs` targets them.
- **The traced/designed tension.** In Round 2 the owner rejected a *traced* end, yet the winning tiles are traces of Ash fitted point by point to his pixels.
  - The owner approved the result ("WAY more improved", "much better").
  - Whether a *new* reference should be traced and fitted, or redesigned as clean tiles, is undecided.

### 6.2 Risks the history raises

1. **Tiles are pinned to one weave.** The designed tiles carry `lam`, `bow`, `arm` and a port phase, and attach only within narrow windows:
   - **λ:** `TILE_LAM_SLACK` = ±12% of the tile's lam (end tile 2.272 band, so about 2.0–2.55; corner tile 2.335).
   - **Bow:** the end tile attaches only within ±0.2 of the bow it was drawn on. The final page runs bow −0.584 against a tile drawn at −0.75: 0.166 off, **0.034 from the cliff** in one direction.
   - **Thickness:** `ash-contained` keeps ink in the frame only at thickness ≤ about 0.45 (the final design uses 0.384).
   - Outside these windows the engine silently falls back to fold caps, the look the owner vetoed. **A "tightness" slider or a "band thickness" slider will fall off this cliff unless tiles are re-authored per λ, parameterised by λ, or generated.**
2. **The fitted tiles encode one drawing's hand.** About 700 centreline points carry per-knot offsets fitted to Ash's pixels (`expt2.disp`), including wobble. They don't transfer to other λ or band, and they produced the cosmetic bumps. Variation needs a tile model with semantic parameters (L leg length, corner sharpness, wrap reach, hairpin depth), not raw displacements.
3. **Pattern coverage is thin.** Only plait-4 (Ash bow) has a finished corner and end. Elsewhere:
   - twist has loop and heart ends, judged OK;
   - plait-3 'ash' keeps one over/under repeat per run, and plait-3 'loop' never shipped;
   - plait-3 and twist corner tiles read as frame folds;
   - openweave, chain and plait-6 have no tiles.
   "Different patterns" means designing a tile set per pattern, plus an over/under seed and colour-role mapping each time.
4. **Colour is phase.** On plait-4 a quarter repeat swaps both a corner's colours and its crossing parity (V7). Any slider that changes λ, band or page size can flip which colour wears the outer L or the wrap, unless `cornerColor` and `endColor` are re-solved. Two colours are assumed throughout; three colours exist only on plait-3, with weaker ends.
5. **Symmetry is a per-reference fact.** `symmetry:'mirror'` needs breaks separating the halves. A closed border (no breaks) is one clockwise sweep with rotation symmetry. Ash's bottom corners are not rotations of TL. A reference with rotational, four-fold or no symmetry needs the self-match test first, and the engine may need modes it lacks.
6. **Page geometry.**
   - Whole-repeat rounding, corner phase pins, `phaseSpread`, `repeatSplit`, `endSlide` and `breakExact` are bookkeeping fitted on Ash's portrait page.
   - The tests cover four page geometries, but the *design* was scored on one.
   - Landscape, other page sizes, other break widths and auto-breaks around emblems will exercise the residual-spreading code in ways not yet seen. The bottom-run stretch is the precedent.
7. **The config surface is fitted, not designed.**
   - About 30 opt-in engine keys accumulated from V4 to V12, plus tool flags.
   - In the final design several sit at roughly zero: `cutCurl` 0.014, `edgeBend` −0.011, `endSlide` 0.004, `fillArcBulge` 0.014, `fillField` coefficients ≤0.009, and `tipExt` is absent.
   - The load-bearing ones: `angular` 0.742, `bow` −0.584, `thickness` 0.384, `repeatLam` 2.324, `cutSlant` 0.970, `fillArcW` 0.950, `fillBarW` 1.037, `fillInk` 1.092, `fillBarInk` 0.954, `endBlend` 1.73, `phaseSpread` 0.80, `OUTLINE_RATIO` 0.517, `GAP_RATIO` 0.533.
   - **A slider UI should expose a handful of perceptual axes** (tightness = λ, band width, strand weight, ink weight, gap, bow or roundness, colours) and derive the bookkeeping keys, not surface them.
8. **The metric is reference-specific.** It depends on a 4-class palette classifier (with about 0.02 of AA-fringe bias), a fixed 1275-wide raster, band-unit region boxes, one primary region per component, and a self-mirror ceiling.
   - Arbitrary references also need automatic palette and role detection, strand-count, repeat and symmetry estimation, box placement, and a ceiling when the art has no self-symmetry.
   - The Ash pipeline took about 10 hours with heavy human direction. A per-point tile fit costs 1–2k renders (3–5 min).
   - The Maglev workaround, the shell-launch rule and a jittered objective are preconditions for trustworthy fits.
9. **Browser performance is unmeasured.** Node renders a warm page in about 35 ms. The fill painter and tile assembly have not been timed in the designer's live-slider loop (the spec's ~30 ms debounce) or through html2canvas export.

### 6.3 What the history suggests for variation (inferences, not decisions)

- **Separate three layers.**
  - **Weave primitives** are already parametric and robust across λ, band and thickness: waveform, alternation, cuts and painter.
  - **Component topology** (corner and end tiles) is designed per pattern.
  - **Component geometry** must be re-derived per λ and thickness.
  The July engine failed by making topology algorithmic. The November engine succeeded by fixing topology, but then also froze geometry to one λ.
- **Make tiles parametric in λ and thickness.** A tile could be a topology plus a few semantic dimensions, re-fitted or rule-generated at the slider's λ. The per-point fitter then serves as a *reference-matching* refinement, not the tile definition. Variation needs tile families, not traced instances.
- **Keep the fitted-page discipline for reference matching:**
  - joint page only;
  - hard gates on every unscored run;
  - jittered objective;
  - a self-mirror ceiling;
  - a visual review each cycle;
  - small-step or per-point search for shape, CMA only for low-dimensional global keys.
- **Treat the owner's principles in §4 as the gate set for slider ranges.** Clamp sliders to where tiles attach, folds stay zero-shear and runs stay unstretched, and never fall back silently.
